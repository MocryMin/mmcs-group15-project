# Opus review request for two Tier 2B report outlines

Ran Min and Codex have proposed two alternative structures for the five pages available to Tier 2B. Please review the outlines independently against the brief and the report marking descriptors, and recommend one outline or a hybrid. This is a review of structure and evidence priorities. Treat the entries below as proposed contents and flag any result whose evidence or comparison would need to be checked.

## Outline 1 · Ran Min's draft

Two parts. Strategy analysis under fixed predictors takes about 3–3.5 pages. The predictors take about 1.5–2 pages.

| Page | Proposed content |
|---|---|
| 1 | First 30% for the selected investment portfolio under policies A and B and an introduction to the operating strategy and results. Next 30% for the forecast targets and horizons. Remaining 40% begins the A/B dispatch method given the forecasts and historical information available at each decision time. |
| 2 | First 20% completes the dispatch method and covers forecast performance. The rest reports economic and sustainability indicators. |
| 3 | First 60% covers performance over time, including six test folds and the investment-size curves under a fixed strategy. Remaining 40% covers additional sensitivities or boundaries. |
| 4 | Diagram of the predictors used in A/B, model and training configurations, and forecast-performance comparisons. |
| 5 | Ablations of the most important predictor configurations, settings or training targets. |

## Outline 2 · Codex's proposed revision

Keep strategy first, with about 3.5 pages for operation and investment and 1.5 pages for forecasting.

| Page | Proposed content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce the portfolios and their connection to Tier 1. Use about 35–40% for that context, the information available when a decision is made and the forecast horizon. Use the remainder to define the half-hour rolling decision, the dispatch constraints and the differences between A and B, including when actions are executed and settled. |
| 2 | One main operating-results table with the zero-battery investment comparator, a simple dispatch policy, the selected policy and a like-for-like perfect-foresight comparator. Show gross operating gain, annualised battery cost, net gain, and operational outcomes such as demand served and unused generation, with renewable attribution where supported. |
| 3 | Give about half a page each to the A/B capacity sweep and the stability of the chosen capacities over six windows. Distinguish the capacity-selection window from the replay of the fixed capacities and state what remains fixed across the sweep. |
| 4 | First half covers seasonal and difficult conditions, the mechanism behind the results, and conditions that change the recommendation, such as battery break-even cost. Second half introduces the predictor interface, shared inputs and A/B outputs in one compact figure, with the essential training and validation design. |
| 5 | Concentrate forecast accuracy here, then show two or three ablations that justify the chosen predictors using both predictive metrics and realised operating value. Close with a short recommendation for A and B and the conditions for changing it. Detailed runs and hyperparameters go in the appendix. |

## Review questions

1. Does each outline tell a coherent story from the Tier 1 investment case through feasible operating decisions to a client recommendation? Identify the point at which a first-time reader would lose track of the equipment, information set, comparator or decision.
2. Which Tier 2B and project-wide requirements would each outline cover or leave weak? In particular, consider cross-period feasibility, the simple-policy comparator, how results vary with seasonal or difficult conditions, renewable use, resilience, and conditions that could change the recommendation. Please distinguish explicit course requirements from your own advice.
3. Is each page realistically sized in the existing academic layout? Identify the most crowded page and suggest precisely what to move, cut or put in the appendix. Point out any analysis that needs to be in the five pages for the conclusion to stand.
4. Recommend one outline or give an amended five-page map. Name the essential tables and figures, and flag the most important evidence or comparison still needed. Please reply to this request in Tier2.
