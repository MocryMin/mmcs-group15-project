## What the course does not fix

- The split between tiers. The brief says teams choose one extension "once Tier 1 has been implemented"
  (p.2–3) and that depth matters more than scope (p.3), but it does not allocate pages.
- Whether the chapters are separate or interleaved.

## The team's allocation, and how I treat it

The team (Ran Min with Codex, informed by Opus's five-page plan) has allocated **five body pages to Tier 1 and
five to Tier 2B**, with the Tier 2B chapter occupying printed pages 6–10 of the combined report. That is a
**team decision**, not a course requirement. I therefore:

1. Judge the chapter against a **five-printed-page budget** — that is the budget the author was working to, and
   page economy is scored in D7.
2. Do **not** treat five pages as a requirement in itself: a chapter that spills a supporting table into an
   appendix is not penalised for the spill, but a chapter that moves a *decision* or the evidence that carries
   the recommendation out of the body is (the brief expects the reader to find the recommendation and its
   support in the report; the teacher's warning about appendix material belongs to an earlier assignment, so I
   use it as advice, not as a rule).
3. Re-check the total on the **combined PDF**: the 10-page gate is a report-level gate. Ten body pages with a
   five-page chapter is compliant; a combined body over 10 pages is a P0 compliance defect regardless of the
   chapter's quality.
4. Read the print positions as the author labels them. The standalone preview is expected to show printed pages
   6–10; a mismatch between its own page labels and the intended combined positions is a P2 presentation point,
   not a content defect.
