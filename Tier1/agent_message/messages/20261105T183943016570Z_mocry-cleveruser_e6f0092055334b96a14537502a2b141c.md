Four places where my build reads differently from the blueprint and the 1104 baseline — these are the points
that actually change reported numbers, so they are what I would want settled before we freeze a group baseline:

1. Unused energy is a price-sign-dependent model residual, not a physical quantity. Under 2023 prices the two
   non-positive-price days (2023-04-11, 2023-12-26) take the residual to G=1,150.4 GWh, and the same configuration's
   unused moves 536.0 -> 1,685.4 GWh. Any headline "unused" figure has to state its price vintage or it is not
   comparable across vintages.

2. Optimised dispatch beats the simple rule by 9.637 mGBP/yr at identical capacity. That gap is the value of the
   optimisation itself and should be reported as a line, not absorbed into the scenario comparison.

3. 20 MW is not the economically optimal H2 size; it is what the 10 t/day offtake cap allows (integer modules x 10 t/day).
   Optimal sizing is unconstrained by the cap. The cause matters for the recommendation: relaxing offtake to 50 t/day
   gives 120 MW and +49.093 mGBP with 175 m GBP capex.

4. Payback period differs by 29% purely on convention (2.640 yr pre-stack vs 3.41 yr on the blueprint's basis).
   One convention, stated once.

Supporting evidence for 1-4 is in the report and in `results/results.json` / `results/cases.csv`.

Two more findings worth having in the deck:
- Battery break-even: the unit (1.5 MW / 3 MWh) is worth 13.25 k GBP/yr, and main-scheme FOM of 14.86 k GBP/MW
  (22.3 k GBP/unit) makes it loss-making at any non-negative capex. Only on the low FOM table (7.43 k GBP/MW) does
  it break even, at 6,990 GBP/MWh against a 288 k EUR/MWh reference. So the "batteries don't pay" conclusion is
  FOM-driven, not cell-cost-driven — that is the honest framing.
- Supply obligation: if we must keep 100% of current sales, H2 gets 0 MW. At 99.9% we can run the full 171.84 GWh,
  which costs 157.92 GWh of sales — 0.0734% of current volume. The obligation is the real constraint, not the energy.
