# Business pitch — Tier 1 talking points (no formulas, no code)

**Audience:** a senior investor on the client's board. **Time:** about two minutes. **Rule:** no equations, no code, no solver output.

## Slide 1 — The opportunity is a contract, not a curtailment problem

- The portfolio produced 215.7 TWh last year against 245.8 TWh of demand; it covers 87.5% of demand.
- 550 GWh is stranded on 29 days when generation exceeds demand. That is the resource.
- A 20 MW electrolyser can absorb at most 0.48 GWh on its best day. The constraint is not the electricity; it is who will buy the hydrogen.

## Slide 2 — What we recommend and what it is worth

- **20 MW of electrolyser, no battery.**
- £29.2m of capital, £8.6m a year of net value, 2.6-year payback before stack replacement, 29% annual return.
- The plant runs at 98% of its maximum annual output, producing 3,074 tonnes of hydrogen a year against a 10-tonne-a-day buyer limit.
- A simple "sell electricity first, then make hydrogen" rule at the same size loses £1.1m a year. The value is in the dispatch, not in the equipment.

## Slide 3 — The honest part: where the electricity comes from

- Only 14 GWh of the plant's 172 GWh input is electricity that would otherwise have gone unused — 8%.
- The other 158 GWh is electricity we would otherwise sell. That is 0.07% of annual sales; it is a small operational concession, but it is a concession.
- If the client insists on selling 100% of current output, the project does not exist. If the licence requires current generation to serve demand first, the project loses money in 2023–24.

## Slide 4 — The conditions and the next step

- We need a buyer for at least 2.2 tonnes a day at £7.80/kg. Below £4.62/kg we do not build. Every £1/kg below the reference costs about £3m a year.
- The size follows the contract: 20 MW for 10 tonnes a day, 120 MW for 50 tonnes a day. We should not pre-build.
- No battery in Tier 1: at the brief's cost, a unit costs about seven times what it earns. If the board wants storage, it needs an intraday study and an ancillary-revenue case.
- **Ask:** authorise the term sheet and the 20 MW order; hold expansion until the contract expands.

## Questions we expect

- *Why not a bigger plant?* Because the buyer, not the wind, sets the size. 40 MW would add capital for at most 1.4 tonnes a day more under a 10-tonne contract.
- *Why not batteries?* At €288k/MWh a 1.5 MW unit costs about £96k a year and earns £13k in our daily model. The model cannot see intraday trading or grid services; that is a separate study, not a reason to buy now.
- *What if hydrogen prices fall?* At £6/kg the 20 MW case still adds £3.3m a year; at £4.62/kg it breaks even; below that we stop.
- *What if the year is bad?* With the size fixed, the value ranged from £6.3m to £16.7m across 2020–2026 under economic dispatch and was positive every year. Under supply-first it was negative in two years.
