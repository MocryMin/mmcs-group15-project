\chapter{Introduction and data}\label{chap:common}
\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
The client wants to know which flexible assets to add to its existing
generation and how to operate them \cite{brief}. Tier 1 appraises batteries
and electrolysers under historical market conditions. The Tier 2B
specification considers operation when future prices, generation and demand
are unknown.

We reconcile the five supplied datasets before modelling. The audit
identifies missing and zero generation records, midnight observations used
as daily averages, low demand readings and inactive price entries.
We rebuild daily values from upstream observations where available and
flag values estimated from other records.

Tier 1 combines market and equipment research with mixed-integer
optimisation. We compare economic dispatch, which allocates electricity by
value, with a rule that serves current electricity demand first. At an
illustrative hydrogen offtake limit of 10 tonnes/day, both select
23.4 MW of electrolysis and zero batteries. Annual gains over the respective
no-investment baselines are \gbp 12.68m and \gbp 2.44m. The analysis tests
when these choices change with offtake, prices and equipment costs.

Tier 2B uses a rolling plan to apply only the current day's decisions
and update the plan as new observations arrive. Forecast accuracy and
operating value are evaluated separately in the design in
Chapter~\ref{chap:tier2}.

\section{Data audit and reconstruction}\label{sec:data}
The inputs are fuel-level generation, its reported total, a simulated wind
farm, demand and market index prices. We align them on 2,452 UTC days from
1 January 2020 to 17 September 2026. Generation and demand are reported as
mean MW and converted to daily MWh. Table~\ref{tab:repair} summarises the
repairs. Appendix~\ref{app:data} documents the evidence, methods and checks.
