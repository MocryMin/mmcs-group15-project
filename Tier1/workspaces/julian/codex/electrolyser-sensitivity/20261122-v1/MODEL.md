# Site-scale dispatch and capacity checks

For each half-hour, existing generation and demand are those in the v7 `flat` wind-farm reconstruction. A's zero-additions comparator sells up to demand at nonnegative prices. Direct electrolysis first takes energy outside that comparator's sales. Additional diverted energy incurs the contemporaneous foregone sale price. B uses positive residual generation after serving demand. The daily contract constraint limits total hydrogen to 10,000 kg per UTC day. The model uses the NEPTUNE V consumption of 55.9 kWh/kg, with 90 kg/hour of nameplate output per module.

The flexible cases optimize each UTC day separately. At fixed installed capacity, the operating load can be zero or lie between one module's minimum load and the installed upper limit, as neighbouring feasible intervals for the number online overlap. The merit-order relaxation is exact whenever it respects that minimum. HiGHS solves days requiring a mixed-integer correction. The flexible baselines match v5 Table C.1 to its reported precision.

For day-boundary commitment, the model calculates the best half-hour load profile for each feasible number of online modules and each day. Dynamic programming carries maturity buckets for new starts across the full chronology, including year boundaries. A module can stop once it has completed its minimum number of days. The horizon starts with all modules off and ends after all starts have completed their required run. For A resizing, the daily cap bounds the number of modules that can remain online at a positive minimum load. Each candidate's best daily commitment with the multi-day restriction removed gives a capacity-screening upper bound. The retained and screened capacities are recorded in the bounds CSV.

For B the surplus test precedes the four- and five-day optimization. The longest half-hour run sufficient for even one module at 20% load is 56 hours. This establishes infeasibility of any positive production in those cases under the surplus-only rule. The script checks the energy balance, daily cap, minimum and maximum loads, minimum-run feasibility and reconstructed sales and hydrogen cash on every solved schedule.

The data provide a historical operating comparison. The chronological solution uses the realised trace and gives a full-information benchmark for the selected operating rules. The four- and five-day cases model restrictions through commitment duration. Additional effects such as startup energy, ramp rates, forced outages or commercial delivery penalties would require separate parameters.
